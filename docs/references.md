# References and claim boundaries

- Ziftci and Greenberg, 2023. [Improving Design Reviews at Google](https://research.google/pubs/improving-design-reviews-at-google/). Supports structured design review in the reported setting; it does not measure this project.
- Liu et al., 2023. [Lost in the Middle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172). Supports selective retrieval as a design consideration; it does not prove all models behave identically.
- Zhang et al., 2024. [Survey on memory mechanisms for LLM agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13501). Provides background on agent-memory mechanisms; it does not establish team productivity gains.
- Schwaber and Sutherland, 2020. [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html). Supplies process terminology for transparency, inspection and adaptation.
- OWASP. [System prompt leakage](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm072025-system-prompt-leakage/). Supports keeping authorization outside prompts.
- METR, 2025. [Developer productivity study](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/). A context-specific counterexample to universal acceleration claims.

The “70/30” claim is not used as a statistic. The cited field notes describe an unmeasured observation about generation and human refinement, not a distribution of team knowledge. The reverse-information-paradox interpretation is also not used to claim planning-time trends. Both may be discussed as hypotheses only after a directly relevant source is verified.
